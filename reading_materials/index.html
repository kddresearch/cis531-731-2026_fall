Course References & Assigned Readings

100% Open-Access Textbooks, Foundational Papers & Ecosystem Documentation

This course relies exclusively on Open-Access and Author-Sanctioned Free-to-Read resources. You do not need to purchase any textbooks or proprietary software subscriptions. Every module pairs a Theoretical Reading (“Why”) with a Practical Systems / API Reference (“How”) aligned with our pinned Python 3.11 / PySpark 3.5 LTS / PyTorch 2.x runtime.

🛡️ Click to Expand: Edition Currency Rules, Pinned Runtime Versions (PySpark 3.5 LTS / PyTorch 2.x) & Citation Policy
  • Edition Currency Guard: Outdated free textbook editions that clash with modern Python 3.11+, PySpark 3.5 LTS, or neural vector search (such as the 2008 edition of Introduction to Information Retrieval or R-based editions of ISLR) are intentionally excluded in favor of the August 19, 2026 draft of Jurafsky & Martin (SLP3) and the 2023 Python edition of ISLP.
  • PySpark 3.5 LTS Documentation Pin: Always consult the Apache Spark 3.5.7 Structured Streaming Programming Guide rather than unpinned /docs/latest/ pages so your API calls match the course DevContainer (`pyspark==3.5.x`, `torch==2.2.1`, `transformers==4.38.2`, `mlflow==2.11.1`, `faiss-cpu==1.8.0`).
  • Two-Tier Container Policy: The pinned CPU DevContainer is the normative baseline for 100% of assignments and grading; the digest-pinned `pytorch/pytorch` CUDA overlay is an optional performance acceleration for validated GPU nodes.

📝 Citation & Redistribution Note

When writing your PS 4, PS 8, and HW 12 Term Project reports, verify that you cite the exact current edition listed above (e.g., ISLP 2023 Python Edition and Jurafsky & Martin SLP3 Aug 2026 Draft). Tier 2 author-hosted texts are provided for direct online reading and classroom study; link to their canonical URLs rather than redistributing PDF copies in public repositories.
