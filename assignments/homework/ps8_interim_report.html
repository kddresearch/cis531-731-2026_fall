1. How to Complete This Milestone

Unlike PS 4, you will not upload a single written PDF for your Interim Report. Instead, you will answer a detailed 8-question Canvas Survey. This format allows us to evaluate your process, methodology, and exploratory embedding analysis alongside your coding progress.

  1. Draft Locally: Read the 8 questions below and draft your answers locally, incorporating your data provenance table, exploratory figures, and architecture details.
  2. Refine with AI: Use the GenAI Kit v3.0 to synthesize and edit your drafted answers. Save your prompt audit log as GenAI.pdf.
  3. Take the Survey: Click the "Take the Survey" button on Canvas to submit your final answers, audit log, and GitHub tag reference.

2. Survey Questionnaire Breakdown (30 Points)

Prepare your answers to these exact prompts before starting the Canvas Survey.

Question 1: Team & Track Metadata (Fill in the Blank / Short Answer)

2 pts

Prompt: List the names of all team members. State your project's Analytical Category (Prescriptive, Descriptive, Predictive), Media Type, claimed OSEMN stages, and your fixed Track (ASR or VDB). CIS 731 teams must state their ontology schema version; CIS 531 teams confirm use of the unmodified instructor schema.

Question 2: Scope Delta and Proposal Responses (Short Answer)

4 pts

Prompt: Provide your updated problem statement. Have your objectives changed or narrowed since your PS 4 Proposal? List every change with justification, and provide a direct response to every instructor proposal comment.

Question 3: Data Provenance & Exploratory Baselines (Short Answers: Table, Figures, Results)

8 pts

Prompt: Provide your data provenance table (corpus/stream source, whitelist ID, size, ingest method; confirming compliance with restricted topics and terms of use license). Include descriptions and findings for at least three exploratory figures, including a UMAP sensitivity sweep over n_neighbors and min_dist (with fixed random_state following Wilke Parts I–II) and a cohort/drift plot with uncertainty. Report your initial advisory performance metric on your naive baseline.

Question 4: Architecture, Invariants & Pillar Alignment (Short Answer)

5 pts

Prompt: Detail your pipeline architecture (ingest, embed/transcribe, index, retrieve/analyze, evaluate) and explain why you chose this approach in alignment with your OSEMN stages. Provide your component-version table that you will include in your final project deliverable as a fixed project manifest and cite your data source and overall objective to allow enforced requirements to be checked.

Question 5: Risk Register and Revised Plan (Short Answer)

5 pts

Prompt: Provide at least 5 quantified risks (likelihood, impact, trigger, and mitigation)—for example, running out of API credits or exceeding your local compute limits, with a CPU-baseline fallback plan ready. Outline your work breakdown and your specific data annotation plan. This must include your 50–200 labeled example target, how you are sourcing pre-annotated data or using automated analytical preprocessing to scale and pre-label unlabeled data into a silver standard, and your Inter-Annotator Agreement (IAA) protocol (such as multi-rater consistency metrics).

Finally, explain your validation strategy, including why keeping a strict fractional cap on your validation set (such as capping validation splits under 1% when working with large scaled datasets) acts as a vital safety criterion to prevent data leakage, overfitting, and evaluation noise.

Authoritative References & Tutorials:
  • Jurafsky & Martin, Speech and Language Processing (3rd ed. draft) — Text Classification, Silver Standards, and Inter-Annotator Agreement
  • James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani, & Taylor (2023), An Introduction to Statistical Learning (ISLP) — Chapter 5 (Validation Strategy, Data Splits, and Resampling Methods)
