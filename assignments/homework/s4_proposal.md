## 1. Overview
For this first milestone, you will define your problem, identify your dataset or environment, and propose a concrete AI methodology aligned with the course taxonomy. 

## 2. Initiating Your Draft (The 5-Stage AI Kit Procedure)
Use the Generative AI Kit to help structure your proposal. Follow these five stages to systematically break down your project:

1. **Pick a Category:** Choose Prescriptive, Descriptive, or Predictive. The GenAI kit is designed to answer concrete questions about *one* prospective project at a time. If you choose a dataset from Kaggle or Hugging Face, be clear whether you are proposing a project category that matches its original intent, or one of your own choosing.
2. **Identify the Data Basis:** Identify the primary source data by volume or velocity. If unsure, present a dataset *(with its schema)* and your category choice to the AI kit. Ask it to analyze the data variety and identify the principal source. If there is more than one topic (e.g., image captioning using vision and text), the source data must be approved by the instructor to formulate a backup plan (e.g., an alternative silver-standard text source if the vision-language model fails).
3. **Specify Scale & Sourcing:** Specify the desired scale of your candidate dataset. Ask the AI to apply the *Data Sourcing Guidelines* (Rubric 06) to define a range of dataset sizes and identify potential ingestion bottlenecks.
4. **Vet the Statement & Distribute the Pipeline:** Ask the AI kit to vet your shared Problem Statement. Then, use it to help distribute the OSEMN pipeline across your team. Outline what each student should write for their Background/Related Work, Methodology, Evaluation Criteria, and Milestones mapping to HW6, HW8 (Interim Report), and HW10 (Final Deliverable).
5. **Assign Crucial Tasks:** Some pipeline stages may be trivial compared to others. Identify the proposed overall task (target output) and assign each *crucial* OSEMN stage to a distinct team member. For interactive dashboards, focus assignments on core (must-have) functionality.

## 3. Required Document Structure
Your proposal must be a **1 to 2-page PDF** containing the following sections exactly as named below. The structure maps directly to the Term Project Taxonomy and Sourcing Guide.

*   **Title & Team:** Project title and the names of all team members (1-3 max).
*   **Taxonomy Alignment:** Explicitly list your target **Category** (Prescriptive, Descriptive, or Predictive) and your target **Topic/Media** (Time-Series, Spatial, Text, Multimedia, or Structured).
*   **Problem Statement:** What is the specific problem you are trying to solve? *(Note: For teams, this is the ONLY section you may share identically).*
*   **Data Sourcing (The Obtain Stage):** Naming a source is not sufficient. You must explicitly name your Tier 1 or Tier 2 source (e.g., Databricks samples, Hugging Face, Open-Meteo), identify the raw landing format (JSON, CSV, Parquet), and state how you will handle authentication/API limits.
*   **Proposed Methodology (The OSEMN Pipeline):** Detail how you will execute the Scrub, Explore, Model, and iNterpret stages. *Teams must explicitly claim which 1-2 stages each individual member is responsible for building natively.* You must also define your "Method 1" (simple/naive) fallback model.
*   **GenAI Audit:** Did you use the Generative AI Kit to synthesize this proposal? You must include your `GenAI.pdf` log or explicitly state "No GenAI Used."

## 4. Grading Rubric (30 Pts)
*   **Scope & Alignment (10 pts):** Clearly identifies a Category and Topic. Problem is well-defined.
*   **Feasibility Check (10 pts):** Algorithms and datasets exist and are explicitly named. Scope is realistic.
*   **Clarity & Format (5 pts):** Follows exact section structure. Submitted as a clean PDF.
*   **GenAI Verification (5 pts):** Includes the required GenAI log or states AI was not used.
