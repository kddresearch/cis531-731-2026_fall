⚠️ Protocol: Individual Proposals, Team Projects

To ensure every student has a rigorous grasp of the project scope, we follow a strict submission protocol:

  • Proposals are INDIVIDUAL: Even if you have formed a team (max 3), EACH member must submit their own PDF proposal.
  • What You Can Share: Team members may copy/paste the same Problem Statement and Background/Related Work sections.
  • What Must Be Unique: Your Methodology and Milestones must reflect your specific role and technical contributions to the effort.
  • Co-Authoring: You may submit shared Interim Reports, Presentations, and Final Codebases/Reports ONLY AFTER your proposal has been approved and your team has been officially registered.

Dimension 1: The 3 Analytical Categories (The Goal)

This dimension defines the core analytical objective and decision framework of the data science system.

Category/Methodology [A]: Prescriptive Analytics (RecSys/IR)

Systems that recommend actions, retrieve specific knowledge (RAG), or automate decision-making based on constraints.

Category/Methodology [B]: Descriptive Analytics (InfoVis/Storytelling)

Systems that expose hidden patterns, generate dashboards, map generational drift, or summarize vast corpora.

Category/Methodology [C]: Predictive Analytics (Classification/Forecasting)

Systems that classify entities (KYC/AML), predict future states (time series), or identify anomalies.

Dimension 2: The 5 Topics / Media Types (The Input)

This dimension specifies the raw modality and structured domain ingested by the data pipeline.

Track/Topic 1: Time-Series / Sequential Data

Financial logs, weather/climate sensors, IoT telemetry, server/traffic logs, defining the technical scope, temporal range, and sequential streaming examples.

Track/Topic 2: Spatial / Georeferenced Data

Epidemiological maps, check-in data, logistics routing, satellite/remote sensing features, defining the spatial coordinates, boundary datasets, and georeferenced examples.

Track/Topic 3: Text Corpora

E-books, conversational transcripts, LLM prompt/response logs, product reviews, historical archives, defining the textual scope, token size, and corpus examples.

Track/Topic 4: Multimedia (Audio/Visual)

ASR streams, caller speech, video captions, music information retrieval (MIR), defining the acoustic/visual scope, sample rates, and streaming examples.

Track/Topic 5: Structured/Relational Data

Complex database queries, web service/API streams, graph network architectures, defining the schema constraints, relational depth, and graph connectivity examples.

Dimension 3: The 5 OSEMN Tasks / Stages (The Execution)

Teams execute a subset of these stages natively and automate the rest.

Task 1: Obtain (Data Ingestion & Extraction)

Prototype: Building a PySpark streaming consumer that attaches to a live Kafka/AWS Kinesis feed of structured API data, or a web scraper that generates a 10GB raw text corpus.
Goal: Reliable, fault-tolerant acquisition of the raw media type.

Task 2: Scrub (Cleaning & Chunking)

Prototype: Implementing recursive character chunking with stride on ASR transcripts, or normalizing/imputing missing values in a massive spatial dataset.
Goal: Transforming raw, noisy inputs into uniform, machine-readable structures ready for embedding or EDA.

Task 3: Explore (EDA & Feature Engineering)

Prototype: Generating UMAP/t-SNE visualizations of semantic embeddings, or building a dashboard that maps the distribution of classes in a time-series log.
Goal: Identifying target variables, understanding latent space clustering, and defining the bounds of the dataset.

Task 4: Model (Machine Learning & Pipelines)

Prototype: Training an XGBoost classifier for transaction fraud, fine-tuning a HuggingFace Transformer for sentiment, or deploying a FAISS/Qdrant Vector Database for semantic search.
Goal: The mathematical core; applying statistical or neural algorithms to execute the Category goal (Predict, Describe, Prescribe).

Task 5: iNterpret (Evaluation & Data Storytelling)

Prototype: Calculating and graphing ROC-AUC, BLEU scores, or semantic coherence metrics, and synthesizing the outputs into an interactive Streamlit or PowerBI dashboard.
Goal: Proving the model's efficacy against baselines and translating statistical outputs into actionable human insights.

Proposal Document Format

Your proposal must be a concise 1–1.5 page PDF structured into the following five required sections:

1. Introduction

  • Problem Statement: Clearly describe the problem you intend to solve within your chosen 3x5x5 coordinate space.
  • Goal: What specific capability or analytical outcome will your system accomplish?
  • Context & Dataset: Define your data source or domain. Clarify whether this is an investigation of novelty (Research) or utility (Application).

2. Background & Related Work

  • Summarize relevant prior work and foundational literature.
  • Requirement: At least 3 citations/references.
  • Requirement: At least one archival/peer-reviewed source (ACM, IEEE, NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR, etc.).

3. Methodology

  • Baselines: What existing method or model architecture will serve as your comparative baseline?
  • Proposed Approach: What novel algorithm, architecture, or OSEMN pipeline integration will you implement?
  • Technical Specifics: Detail your modeling framework, training objective, algorithms, and software libraries. Avoid vague assertions.

4. Evaluation Plan

  • Quantitative Metrics: Accuracy, F1-score, BLEU, ROC-AUC, reward convergence, or latency benchmarks.
  • Qualitative Assessment: Error analysis, sample generation audits, or interpretive case studies.
  • Validation Rigor: Train/test splits, cross-validation protocols, and statistical hypothesis testing.

5. Milestones & Timeline

  • List 3–6 dated objectives corresponding to your claimed OSEMN pipeline stages.
